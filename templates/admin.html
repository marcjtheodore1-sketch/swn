🛟 If someone needs more support than we can offer

You are a walk leader and host, not a carer. You're not expected to provide personal care, one-to-one support or supervision — and you're not expected to judge whether someone needs a carer. That isn't your call to make.

Before the walk: open the walk's registrations and read the “Attending With” column. Anyone attending with a carer or support worker, and anyone who has told us who is responsible for them, will show there along with a contact mobile number. Save those numbers to your phone before you set off.

What to do in the moment: do what feels reasonable and kind, keep yourself and everyone else safe, and don't take on more than you're comfortable with. You don't have to solve it alone — you always have a second volunteer, so one of you can stay with the group while the other helps. If someone came with a carer or support worker, go to them first: supervision is their responsibility, not yours. If anyone is in danger or needs urgent medical help, call 999.

Afterwards, write down what actually happened as soon as you can — what you saw and did, not conclusions about the person.

Then tell Chris and Kirsty — Chris is our Designated Safeguarding Lead and Kirsty is our internal safeguarding expert. They'll follow it up properly.

Attendees are told the same thing before they book: our volunteers can't provide personal care, one-to-one support or supervision, and anyone who needs that must bring someone with them who provides it. You are never obliged to fill that gap on the day.
